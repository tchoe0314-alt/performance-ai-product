# Free invitation-only pilot: founder decisions and operating draft

Founder decisions from this conversation:

- The first pilot is free and invitation-only. Do not enable charging, public self-service registration or paid-provider spending as part of this preparation.
- Locations remain open. A representative test jurisdiction is not a location restriction. Jurisdiction and accepted local requirements belong to each project; missing or unsupported requirements remain unknown/unverified, not automatically compliant.
- The founder will arrange the pilot firm and professional/CAD reviewers. Recruitment is not an agent task or a prerequisite to continuing safe software work. Their eventual acceptance remains separate evidence.

## Initial supported-use draft

One parcel, an office/retail concept, a primary building, access and surface parking, entered constraints, supported preliminary calculations/quantities, and review-only PDF/editable DXF. Customer-supplied prices need recorded sources; absent prices remain unknown. Do not advertise automatic all-jurisdiction compliance, native DWG, a professional seal, construction approval or verified survey control.

Existing drawing-only export ceilings are 500 objects and 3,000 vertices. These are rejection limits, not proof that every feature performs acceptably at that size. Three local small-commercial benchmark iterations passed at approximately 6.6–6.9 seconds per run and 121 MB peak memory. Do not turn this single-host measurement into a hosted latency, concurrent-user or larger-project promise. Start with one controlled project/workload before increasing usage.

## Before the first firm receives access

1. Record the exact deployed release and post-deploy login, tenant, save/reopen, calculation/worker and export evidence.
2. Record the permitted preliminary-use scope and known limitations. Require that users understand stale/unknown results and professional-review boundaries.
3. Confirm the existing support contact is reachable, name the support/escalation owner and agree response expectations. Do not publish an unstaffed response-time promise.
4. Decide which confidential information may be uploaded, who may access it for support, retention/deletion expectations and the exit/export process. Use the existing lifecycle controls and verify their operation.
5. Have the founder and appropriate human reviewer approve actual pilot terms/privacy language before publication or acceptance. This file is an internal operating draft, not a contract or legal approval.
6. Provision only approved participants, keep public registration disabled on the hosted service, test role boundaries and confirm how access is paused or removed.

The existing `docs/pilot-business-operations.md` packet remains the detailed process. Paid-provider/payment checks are not a prerequisite to this free offer; professional responsibility, permissions, privacy, reliability and truthful output checks still apply.
